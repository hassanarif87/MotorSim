# Feedforward current controller

These equations describe [current_controller.py](current_controller.py): a continuous-time PI current controller with feedforward, a voltage limit, and anti-windup.

The **d axis** follows the rotor's magnetic field. The **q axis** is perpendicular to it and controls torque in this motor model.

## Signals and parameters

| Symbol | Code name | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $i_d^*, i_q^*$ | `id_ref`, `iq_ref` | Requested currents (A) |
| $i_d, i_q$ | `i_d`, `i_q` | Measured currents (A) |
| $\omega$ | `omega` | Mechanical rotor speed (rad/s) |
| $n_p$ | `np` | Number of pole pairs |
| $L$ | `Lq` | Inductance of both axes, assuming $L_d=L_q$ (H) |
| $K_e$ | `Ke` | Back-EMF constant, defined against mechanical speed (V per rad/s) |
| $K_p, K_i, K_{aw}$ | `Kp`, `Ki`, `Kaw` | Proportional, integral, and anti-windup gains |
| $x_d, x_q$ | `x[0]`, `x[1]` | Integral voltage contributions (V) |
| $V_{bus}$ | `V_bus` | DC supply voltage (V) |

The input order is `[id_ref, iq_ref, id, iq, omega]`; the output order is `[vd, vq]`.

## 1. Current error

$$
e_d=i_d^*-i_d, \qquad e_q=i_q^*-i_q
$$

The error is how far each measured current is below its target.

## 2. Feedforward voltage

Electrical speed is mechanical speed multiplied by the number of pole pairs:

$$
\omega_e=n_p\omega
$$

The controller adds these voltages before applying the voltage limit:

$$
v_{d,ff}=-\omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
v_{q,ff}=\omega_e L i_d+K_e\omega
$$

The terms containing $L$ compensate for coupling between the two current axes as the rotor turns. The $K_e\omega$ term compensates for the voltage generated by the spinning motor (back EMF). In this implementation, back EMF uses **mechanical** speed.

For context, the matching motor equations are

$$
L\frac{di_d}{dt}=v_d-R_m i_d+\omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
L\frac{di_q}{dt}=v_q-R_m i_q-\omega_e L i_d-K_e\omega.
$$

Adding the feedforward terms cancels the speed-dependent terms when the model parameters match and the voltage is not limited. The PI part supplies the remaining voltage, including the resistive drop; there is no separate resistance feedforward term.

## 3. PI plus feedforward

$$
v_{d,raw}=K_p e_d+x_d+v_{d,ff}
$$

$$
v_{q,raw}=K_p e_q+x_q+v_{q,ff}
$$

The proportional term reacts immediately to current error. The integral term builds up over time to remove a persistent error. Feedforward anticipates the voltage needed because the motor is rotating.

## 4. Voltage limit

The circular SVPWM voltage limit is

$$
V_{lim}=\frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt{3}}, \qquad
r=\sqrt{v_{d,raw}^2+v_{q,raw}^2}.
$$

The implementation computes

$$
s=\min\left(1,\frac{V_{lim}}{\max(r,10^{-12})}\right),
\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}v_d\\v_q\end{bmatrix}
=s\begin{bmatrix}v_{d,raw}\\v_{q,raw}\end{bmatrix}.
$$

If the requested voltage is too large, both components shrink by the same factor. This keeps the voltage direction while limiting its magnitude. The small denominator guard avoids division by zero.

## 5. Integral update and anti-windup

$$
\frac{dx_d}{dt}=K_i e_d+K_{aw}(v_d-v_{d,raw})
$$

$$
\frac{dx_q}{dt}=K_i e_q+K_{aw}(v_q-v_{q,raw})
$$

Without limiting, the anti-windup terms are zero and the states simply integrate current error. When voltage is limited, these terms pull the integral states toward the voltage the inverter can provide. This prevents excessive integral buildup while a target is unreachable.
